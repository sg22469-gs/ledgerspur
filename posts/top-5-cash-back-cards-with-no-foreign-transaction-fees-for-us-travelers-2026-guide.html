Among the most egregious is the foreign transaction fee—an automatic surcharge of 1% to 3% added to every purchase made outside the United States. If you spend $3,000 on hotels, dining, and shopping in Tokyo or London, a standard credit card could quietly extract up to $90 in unnecessary fees.

Pairing zero foreign transaction fees with high-value cash back rewards flips the script. Instead of losing 3% on every swipe, you end up earning up to 3% back on your international expenses. How to Choose & Insider Strategy for Spending Abroad

Maximizing rewards while traveling requires more than just picking a card from a list. To ensure you never pay hidden foreign exchange markups, follow this step-by-step framework:

  1. Verify the Network and Acceptance Rate: Visa and Mastercard enjoy near-universal acceptance globally. While American Express and Discover have expanded coverage, smaller foreign merchants and transit hubs outside the US predominantly process Visa and Mastercard.
  2. Decline "Dynamic Currency Conversion" (DCC): When paying with a credit card abroad, point-of-sale terminals or ATMs may ask if you wish to be charged in US Dollars (USD) or local currency (e.g., Euros, Yen, Pounds). Always choose the local currency. Choosing USD triggers DCC, allowing the merchant's foreign bank to convert the currency at exorbitant exchange rates—often adding an extra 3% to 7% hidden fee.
  3. Establish a Two-Card Portfolio Strategy: Combine a flat-rate card (like the Fidelity Visa or Capital One Quicksilver) with a category multiplier (like the Wells Fargo Autograph or Capital One Savor). Use the category card for meals, transportation, and lodging, and fall back on the flat-rate card for boutique shopping or entrance fees.
  4. Set Travel Notifications & Mobile Wallet Integration: Most major US issuers no longer require explicit travel notices, but ensuring your card is synced to Apple Pay or Google Pay provides contactless convenience and added tokenized security overseas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do cash back credit cards charge foreign transaction fees?

Many standard cash back credit cards charge a foreign transaction fee of 2.7% to 3% on purchases processed outside the US. However, issuers like Capital One never charge these fees on any of their cards, and specific products from Wells Fargo, Fidelity, and Chase also waive them completely. Always check the card's pricing and terms sheet under "Foreign Transaction Fee."

Is the Capital One Quicksilver good for international travel?

Yes. The Capital One Quicksilver is an excellent international travel card because it charges $0 in foreign transaction fees, has $0 annual fee, earns a flat 1.5% cash back on all international transactions, and operates on Visa/Mastercard networks with global acceptance.

What happens if I select USD instead of local currency at a foreign register?

Selecting USD activates Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC). The foreign merchant's payment provider converts the purchase amount using an inflated exchange rate that benefits the vendor, effectively stripping away the financial advantages of holding a no-foreign-transaction-fee card.

Final Verdict

Traveling internationally shouldn't mean sacrificing cash back rewards or paying unnecessary bank surcharges. If you want a zero-annual-fee card that maximizes cash back on dining and entertainment, the Capital One Savor Cash Rewards is our top choice. For effortless, flat-rate earnings across all domestic and international spending, the Fidelity Visa Signature or Capital One Quicksilver are reliable, high-performing options.
