That extra charge quickly wipes out whatever 1.5% or 2% cash back you earned in the first place.

Special Note: Pure Cash Back vs. Chase Sapphire Series

When searching for non-FTF cards, travelers frequently consider the Chase Sapphire Preferred Card. It is essential to distinguish between pure cash back cards and premium travel cards:

  • The Chase Reality: Pure Chase cash back cards like the Chase Freedom Unlimited and Chase Freedom Flex charge a 3% foreign transaction fee. Using them abroad erases your earnings.
  • The Chase Sapphire Alternative: The Chase Sapphire Preferred waives foreign transaction fees entirely and earns 3x points on dining and 2x on general travel. While points can be redeemed for cash back at 1 cent per point (e.g., 10,000 Ultimate Rewards points = $100 cash back), the card carries a $95 annual fee. If your goal is strictly $0-annual-fee, pure cash back, sticky options like Capital One or Wells Fargo are better choices.

How to Choose & Insider Strategy for International Spending

To maximize your return when traveling abroad, follow this four-step strategy to ensure you do not pay unnecessary fees while getting the maximum cash back value:

  1. Audit Card Foreign Fee Policies: Always verify the terms of your cards before leaving the U.S. Look specifically for "Foreign Transaction Fee" under the Pricing and Terms schedule. It must state "None" or "0%".
  2. Always Pay in Local Currency (Avoid Dynamic Currency Conversion): When paying with a credit card overseas, payment terminals often ask whether you want to pay in U.S. Dollars (USD) or the local currency (e.g., EUR, GBP, JPY). Always choose the local currency. Choosing USD triggers Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC), where the foreign merchant's bank applies an unfavorable exchange rate that can charge an extra 3% to 7% in fees, bypassing your credit card's zero-FTF protection.
  3. Pair Network Types: Visa and Mastercard enjoy near-universal acceptance globally. While American Express and Discover have broadened their international reach, some European and Asian merchants still decline them. Carry at least one zero-FTF Visa or Mastercard.
  4. Combine a Category Card with a Flat-Rate Card: Use a card like the U.S. Bank Altitude Go or Capital One Savor for 3%–4% back on dining, and fall back on the Fidelity Rewards Visa Signature or Capital One Quicksilver for 1.5%–2% back on shopping, souvenirs, and transportation.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is a foreign transaction fee and how much does it cost?

A foreign transaction fee is a surcharge levied by credit card issuers—typically 1% to 3% of the total purchase amount—on transactions that are processed outside the United States or in foreign currencies. On a $3,000 trip overseas, a 3% foreign fee adds an extra $90 in hidden costs to your credit card statement.

Why do cash back cards like the Chase Freedom charge foreign transaction fees?

Card issuers use foreign transaction fees to offset the costs of international cross-border payment processing and currency conversion. Many popular cash back cards keep their domestic rewards high and annual fees at $0 by applying a 3% fee on foreign transactions. However, banks like Capital One and select cards from Wells Fargo, Fidelity, and U.S. Bank waive these fees entirely on specific credit cards.

Can I use cash back earned abroad for statement credits?

Yes. Cash back earned on international purchases behaves exactly like rewards earned inside the U.S. You can redeem your earnings for statement credits, direct deposits into linked checking or savings accounts, or paper checks, depending on the bank's rules.

Do debit cards charge foreign transaction fees when withdrawing cash abroad?

Most standard bank debit cards charge a 1% to 3% foreign transaction fee plus out-of-network ATM fees when used overseas. If you need cash abroad, consider debit cards specifically designed for travel, such as the Schwab Bank High Yield Investor Checking Account debit card, which waives foreign transaction fees and reimburses all global ATM fees unlimitedly.
