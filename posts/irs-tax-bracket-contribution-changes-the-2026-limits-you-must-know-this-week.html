Strategic Blueprint: How to Maximize the Updated Limits

Updating your wealth strategy requires structured execution. Follow this step-by-step framework to optimize your tax position and retirement contributions across current and upcoming tax years.

  1. Recalculate Per-Paycheck 401(k) Deferrals: Divide the updated annual employee contribution cap by your total remaining pay periods. Ensure your contribution rate captures 100% of employer matching funds without maxing out prematurely—unless your plan sponsor offers a "true-up" provision.
  2. Assess SECURE 2.0 Super Catch-Up Eligibility: If you reach age 60, 61, 62, or 63 during the tax year, audit your plan provisions. SECURE 2.0 allows an elevated catch-up limit for this specific bracket. Note that high earners (making over $145,000 in prior-year FICA wages) must make catch-up contributions into a Roth 401(k) account.
  3. Evaluate Your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) Phase-Outs: Review current IRS income phase-out ranges for Roth IRA contributions. If your household income exceeds the threshold, fund a non-deductible Traditional IRA and immediately convert it into a Roth IRA (Backdoor Roth) via custodians like Fidelity or Schwab.
  4. Max Out Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) First: The HSA remains the single most tax-efficient vehicle available—offering a triple tax advantage (tax-deductible contributions, tax-free growth, and tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses). Ensure your annual contributions are set to hit the maximum self-only or family cap.
  5. Prepare for Potential TCJA Sunset Shifts: If current individual income tax rates expire and revert to pre-2018 brackets, your marginal bracket could increase. Balance pre-tax tax-deferred contributions (Traditional 401(k)) with post-tax accounts (Roth 401(k)/IRA) to build tax flexibility for retirement withdrawals.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the updated IRS 401(k) contribution limits?

The IRS adjusts 401(k) contribution limits annually based on cost-of-living indices. The individual elective deferral limit allows employees to contribute up to the updated cap, while total combined contributions (employee + employer match + profit sharing) remain subject to overall plan limitations. Workers aged 50 and older can make additional catch-up contributions, with enhanced limits applying to ages 60–63 under SECURE 2.0 provisions.

How does the SECURE 2.0 "Super Catch-Up" work for older workers?

Under SECURE 2.0 rules, individuals aged 60 to 63 gain access to an expanded catch-up contribution cap instead of the standard age-50+ catch-up limit. However, workers earning above $145,000 in FICA wages from the plan sponsor in the preceding calendar year are required to make all catch-up contributions on a Roth (after-tax) basis.

How do I execute a Backdoor Roth IRA if my income exceeds IRS limits?

If your Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) crosses the phase-out limit for direct Roth IRA contributions, you can make a non-deductible contribution to a traditional IRA up to the annual limit. Once settled, convert those funds to a Roth IRA. Ensure you consider the IRS pro-rata rule if you maintain existing pre-tax balances in any non-Roth IRAs (SEP, SIMPLE, or Traditional IRAs).
