💡 Key Takeaways

  • Yes, bank bonuses are fully taxable: The IRS treats checking, savings, and brokerage cash promotions as taxable interest income (Form 1099-INT) or unearned income (Form 1099-MISC), not tax-free gifts.
  • The $10 threshold trap: Financial institutions are required to issue a 1099-INT if your earnings equal $10 or more, but taxpayers are legally obligated to report all bonus income on Form 1040, even if no tax form is received.
  • Credit card vs. Bank promos: Spend-based credit card rewards are non-taxable rebates, whereas deposit-based or direct-deposit bank account bonuses are 100% taxable at your standard marginal income tax rate.

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) categorizes cash bank bonuses as interest income rather than tax-free rebates. Whether you earned $50 from a Marcus by Goldman Sachs referral link or a $1,000 cash deposit reward from a major commercial bank, you must account for these payouts on your tax return. The IRS Rule: Why Bank Account Bonuses Are Taxed as Interest

To understand why bank account bonuses are taxable, it helps to review IRS Section 61, which defines gross income as "all income from whatever source derived." The IRS makes a critical distinction between reward models:

  • Deposit & Direct Deposit Bonuses (Taxable): When a bank gives you cash to open an account, deposit funds, or set up a recurring direct deposit (e.g., Marcus, SoFi, Chase), the IRS views that compensation as interest paid to secure your capital. Because you did not have to make a retail purchase to earn it, the payment is treated as standard unearned interest income.
  • Credit Card Spend Bonuses (Non-Taxable Rebates): If you receive $200 back after spending $1,000 on a credit card, the IRS views that money as a price reduction or rebate on your purchases. Cash rebates are not taxable because you had to spend money to receive them.

Form 1099-INT vs. Form 1099-MISC

Most bank promotions are reported on Form 1099-INT (Interest Income) in Box 1. However, some institutions occasionally report non-deposit gifts (such as physical merchandise, premium electronics, or referral cash payments) on Form 1099-MISC (Miscellaneous Information) in Box 3 under "Other Income." Regardless of which form arrives in your mailbox or online portal, both categories are subject to ordinary federal and state income tax rates.

  • State and local income taxes apply to 1099-INT earnings in addition to federal taxes.

Evaluating Key Banking Institutions & Tax Delivery

When collecting cash bonuses across financial institutions, receiving accurate tax documents on time is essential for hassle-free tax filing.

1. Marcus by Goldman Sachs

Marcus is a favorite for high-yield savings due to its competitive baseline APY and its popular referral program, which awards an extra 1.00% APY boost for three months per successful referral. Marcus aggregates all interest earned from APY yields alongside bonus rates and reports them on a single Form 1099-INT. If your combined earnings across Marcus accounts reach $10 or more in a calendar year, your 1099-INT is posted to your online account document hub by late January.

2. SoFi Bank

SoFi regularly offers tiered checking and savings cash bonuses (often $50 to $300) when customers set up qualifying direct deposits within an evaluation window. SoFi lumps the bonus reward into your total interest income. This means your cash promo payout will appear directly in Box 1 of your SoFi 1099-INT alongside any APY accrued on your balance during the year.

3. Capital One 360

Capital One 360 runs substantial promotional campaigns for new high-yield savings and checking customers, requiring designated cash deposits held for 90 days. Capital One delivers 1099-INT forms electronically via their online banking dashboard and mails physical copies to accounts that have opted out of paperless delivery. Their tax reporting is reliable and integrates directly into leading tax software.

How to Report Form 1099-INT Using Popular Tax Software

Reporting your promotional earnings accurately prevents IRS mismatch notices (such as CP2000 letters) down the road. Here is how the top tax preparation tools handle 1099-INT inputs for bank account bonuses.

Filing Bank Bonuses on TurboTax

TurboTax simplifies reporting 1099-INT income by offering automated API imports for major banks including Marcus, Capital One, Chase, and Citi.

  1. Navigate to the Wages & Income section of your tax return.
  2. Select Interest on 1099-INT and click Start or Revisit.
  3. Choose your financial institution from the search bar to import data automatically, or select "Type it in myself."
  4. Enter the bank's Name and Payer's Federal Identification Number (FIN/EIN).
  5. Input the cash bonus amount in Box 1: Interest Income.
  6. If you received a bonus under $10 without a formal 1099-INT, select the prompt for "Unreported Interest Income" to manually add the payout to your total.

Filing Bank Bonuses on H&R Block

H&R Block offers similar auto-import capabilities for high-yield savings and checking promotions.

  1. Go to the Income tab and select Interest (1099-INT).
  2. Upload your PDF tax document, use the electronic import feature, or manually enter the details.
  3. Ensure the dollar amount matches Box 1 of your paper or digital form.
  4. If your bonus was issued on Form 1099-MISC instead of 1099-INT, navigate to Other Income and report it under Line 8z of Schedule 1 (Form 1040).

Step-by-Step Checklist for Bank Bonus Churners

If you plan to earn multiple cash account promotions this year, follow this strategy to keep your tax liability low and your record-keeping organized:

  1. Maintain a Promotion Spreadsheet: Track every account opened, the opening date, deposit requirements, the exact bonus payout date, and the net cash received.
  2. Calculate Your Effective After-Tax Yield: Multiply your cash bonus by your marginal tax rate to find your true return. For example, a $400 bonus earned by a filer in the 24% federal tax bracket and 5% state bracket leaves $284 in net earnings:

    $400 × (1 - 0.24 - 0.05) = $284 Net Return

  3. Watch out for Account Early-Closure Fees: Banks often require accounts to remain open for 6 to 12 months post-bonus payout. Closing an account early can result in a clawback of your cash bonus, even after a 1099-INT has already been reported to the IRS.
  4. Report All Income: Even if a bank does not mail you a Form 1099-INT because your total interest was $8.50, you are still legally required to include that $8.50 on Schedule B or Form 1040 Line 2b.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are bank bonuses taxable 1099 INT income in all 50 states?

Yes. Bank bonuses reported on Form 1099-INT are treated as interest income at both the federal and state levels. Unless you reside in a state without personal income tax (e.g., Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington), you must report the bonus on your state income tax filing alongside your federal Form 1040.

What happens if I didn't receive a Form 1099-INT from my bank?

Financial institutions are only required by federal law to generate and send a Form 1099-INT if the total interest/bonus paid to you equals $10 or more. However, IRS rules state that taxpayers must report all taxable interest earned during the tax year, regardless of whether a 1099-INT form was generated by the bank.

What is the difference between credit card cash-back taxes and bank bonus taxes?

Credit card cash-back and point rewards that require you to make transactions are classified by the IRS as purchase rebates, making them tax-free. In contrast, bank account sign-up bonuses, high-yield deposit boosts, and savings promotions do not require spend transactions, so the IRS taxes them as interest income.

Can a bank bonus push me into a higher tax bracket?

Yes. Bank account bonuses increase your overall Adjusted Gross Income (AGI). Under the US progressive tax system, entering a higher bracket only applies the higher rate to income earned above the bracket threshold, not your entire income.

Final Verdict & Next Steps

Collecting cash promotions from financial institutions remains one of the lowest-risk ways to earn extra yield on your cash reserves. However, you must factor in taxes when evaluating any promotion. Always assume that cash deposit rewards will yield a 1099-INT form, track your payouts in real time, and report your earnings accurately using tax filing tools like TurboTax or H&R Block.
